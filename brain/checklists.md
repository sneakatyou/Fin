Research for Shashank's own decisions, not advice. Written 18 Oct 2026. Nothing here changes a rule.

## 1. How to use this page

- These are lenses, not triggers. An item asks a question; it never creates a trade, trigger or rule.
- The Strategy Playbook, Agent Mandate and Collateral and Stress Test govern. On any conflict, follow them and raise it at the weekly review.
- Anything new (rule, field, threshold) is a proposal for the weekly review. Section 9 lists the open ones; runs do not apply them.
- Answer items in the setup, pulse or review note. Tags name the Brain page with the detail. The 5-16 Oct lessons are Anecdotal (ten sessions, one regime).

## 2. Before freezing any intraday setup

1. Re-test the regime label against the last five closes and date it. "Nine-week downtrend" outlived two up weeks and produced five shorts in a row. \[Retrospective\]
2. Find the prior-day high and low before freezing levels. \[Retrospective\]
3. State the R still available from the first permitted entry. A fade finished by 09:40 has no R left at 09:45. \[Traders: Crabel\] \[Retrospective\]
4. Write the arm level as a distance from the prior close; check the pre-market picture supports that move. A failed-breakout short needs a breakout attempt; an oversold reversal needs a flush. \[Retrospective\]
5. Ask whether it is the same bet as the collateral (index, metals, PSU banks). If yes, say so; the trigger must still do the work. \[Retrospective\] \[Quant\]
6. Price the stop as it will fill. A close-based stop gaps through (MU: about -1.5R against 1R planned). \[Retrospective\]
7. Name the family's outside-evidence rating; India is untested (ORB Weak, failed breakout Anecdotal). \[Evidence\]
8. Write round-trip cost in R; tight stops make fixed costs larger. \[Traders: sizing\] \[Quant\]
9. Write a GO or SKIP verdict with one reason before the open, even when no setup is frozen. \[Retrospective\] \[Quant\]

## 3. Before a swing trade

1. Give it one label. A thesis needing a re-rating by a date is a catalyst trade, not Core. \[Investors: PSU banks\]
2. Name the revision, contract or catalyst behind the move. Leaders with contracts ran (VST, CEG), not sympathy names. \[Retrospective\] \[Investors: Mauboussin\]
3. Write the stop as a price and in R, gap-aware. Most swing rejects had no priced stop. \[Retrospective\]
4. State the horizon: momentum pays over weeks to months, reversal over days. \[Traders: Trend\]
5. Note whether the index is within 10 sessions of a multi-week low; momentum crashes cluster in panic rebounds (Strong, US stocks). \[Evidence\]
6. Count correlated names as one bet. Ten of 12 marked swing rejects fell in one AI hardware and credit week. \[Retrospective\]
7. Judge at the pre-set horizon, not day one. MRVL, AAOI and LEU adds looked fine on day one, then fell 11%, 29% and 19% in a week. \[Retrospective\]

## 4. Before buying or adding to a long-term position

1. What is my conservative value range, and how far below its low end is the price? Cheap without profitability is a value trap. \[Investors: Graham, Klarman\] \[Evidence\]
2. What are owner earnings after capex needed to stand still? (Indian Bank: price to book against sustainable ROE.) \[Investors: Buffett\]
3. What growth, margin and years does the price already imply? \[Investors: Mauboussin\]
4. How would this fail? Write the invalidation first. \[Investors: Munger\]
5. Which Lynch category, and what ends the holding? \[Investors: Lynch\]
6. Where are we in the cycle; what is the margin on normal, not peak, earnings? \[Investors: Marks, Lynch\]
7. Who is a forced or uninterested seller, and is that why it is cheap? \[Investors: Greenblatt, Klarman\]
8. Is the downside permanent, and could a forced sale make it so? \[Investors: Marks, Klarman\]
9. Is it independent of what I hold, or part of a cluster? \[Investors: Pabrai, Fisher\]
10. Would I rebuild this position, at this size, today, from the after-tax cash of selling something else? \[Investors: Fisher, Bogle\]

Indian names, in addition:

1. Promoter check from latest shareholding and SAST filings: pledge level, trend and reason; cash upstreamed to the parent; related-party flows; auditor changes. A fail means smaller size or none, not a lower target. \[Investors: Kedia, Bakshi\]
2. Does the promoter borrow against the stock we post as collateral? Two claimants on one asset. \[Investors: pledge studies\] \[Evidence\]
3. Test each Vedanta entity on its own: cycle, debt split, governance, index-selling overhang. \[Investors: spin-offs\]

## 5. Reviewing a holding and deciding to rotate

1. Confirm one Playbook label and one Lynch category; the category drives the exit. \[Investors: Lynch\]
2. Cyclical: exit on peak earnings and cycle position, not P/E. Fast grower: when growth slows. Compounder: only on thesis damage or a clearly better use of money. \[Investors: Lynch, Fisher\]
3. Confirm a written invalidation exists: the price, event or data point that falsifies the thesis. If none, say so. \[Retrospective\]
4. Remember the INDmoney book: AAOI -31.1%, RKLB -25.5%, IREN -23.3% in the week to 16 Oct, all held with no written invalidation. Filters stopped adds, not losses in holdings. \[Retrospective\]
5. Separate a fall from a broken thesis. Name the dated test (INTC Q3 on 29 Oct). \[Retrospective\]
6. Run the rebuild-from-cash test, per Vedanta entity. "Yes, but smaller" means trim. \[Investors: Fisher\]
7. Rotate only into something materially better after tax, costs and (US) the LRS hurdle, not because a correction is expected. \[Investors: Fisher\] \[Quant\]

## 6. Sizing and leverage against pledged collateral

The Playbook's collateral list governs. Add these checks.

1. Count risk by factor, not ticker. About 57% of the pledged book is Vedanta group plus Indian Bank, so index, metal and PSU-bank longs are the same bet. \[Quant\] \[Traders: sizing\]
2. Define ruin as a margin shortfall or forced sale of pledged core holdings; size for stress-case headroom. \[Traders: sizing\]
3. Pledged stock is credited at value less a haircut; both can move against you together. Check the broker's haircut list. \[Traders: sizing\]
4. Say which way the book moves: a squeeze lifts collateral and hurts shorts; a crash reverses it. A NIFTY short offsets only as far as the book tracks NIFTY. \[Traders: sizing\]
5. Size from the stop (rupee risk divided by stop distance), never from a target or the Rs 10k-50k aspiration. \[Traders: sizing\]
6. Check ten straight losses are survivable: about 9.6% of equity at 1% risk per trade, 18.3% at 2%. \[Traders: sizing\]
7. Fund valuation-dislocation ideas and live experiments from staged cash, not pledged leverage. \[Quant\]

## 7. Scoring and statistics hygiene

1. Report N with every rate. A win rate from 30 trades is about plus or minus 18 points; from 100, about 10. \[Retrospective\]
2. Report trigger rate per family with its denominator. Zero in six fits a true rate up to about 39%. \[Retrospective\] \[Quant\]
3. Do not call an edge on tens of trades: +0.2R needs about 330 trades, +0.1R about 610 (80% power), and regime splits multiply that. Thirty can flag a process problem, never promote. \[Evidence\]
4. Count variants tried: thirteen hypotheses give about a 26% chance one looks significant by luck. A changed rule is a new hypothesis. \[Evidence\] \[Quant\]
5. Forward only. Anything found by looking back is a hypothesis, never a sample; 5-16 Oct data counts toward none. \[Retrospective\]
6. Rejects are not independent samples; keep them apart from live P&L. \[Retrospective\] \[Evidence\]
7. Label hindsight: "avoided loss" and "missed gain" are hindsight by construction. Fix thresholds in R beforehand. \[Retrospective\]
8. Mark scores Full, Approximate or Reconstructed. Never widen an entry zone after the answer is known. \[Retrospective\]
9. Assume negative expectancy after costs until the Ledger shows otherwise (SEBI: about 71% of intraday cash and 91% of F&O individuals lost). Haircut published edges by half or more. \[Evidence\]

## 8. Red flags: no trade today

- The regime label is stale, or the prior-day high or low was not found. \[Retrospective\]
- The trigger needs a flush or breakout attempt nothing pre-market suggests. \[Retrospective\]
- No R is left after the first permitted entry. \[Traders: Crabel\]
- The entry zone is narrower than observable prices, or option prices are unavailable. \[Retrospective\]
- The idea was built after seeing what price did, or treats a gap as the trigger. \[Retrospective\]
- A rupee target or a same-day loss to recover is driving size. \[Traders: Coval and Shumway\]

No trade is a valid result. Log the GO or SKIP verdict and the reason. \[Investors: Munger\]
