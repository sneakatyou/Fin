## The short version

1. "13%" can mean two very different things. As a total gain over 4 to 5 years it is 2.5% to 3.1% a year, below a fixed deposit and below inflation. As an annualised return (XIRR or CAGR) it is ahead of the Nifty 50 (about 7% to 8% a year with dividends over the same windows, Inference), the flexi-cap category average (11.46% over 5 years) and an FD. We do not know which, so every figure is shown both ways.
2. The statement settles it: get the CAS or the INDmoney connector first.
3. Anything measured now is measured near a low. The Nifty closed at 23,146.15 on 16 October, 10.9% below its 19 December 2025 close of 25,966.40 (S3, S4). His notes put the 2026 low at 22,179.90 on 8 October.
4. "Not growing much" is not a reason by itself. Three levers, in order: regular versus direct plan, each fund against its benchmark on rolling returns, and the role the money plays.
5. Role matters most. Rs 8.5 lakh is about 21% of his Indian equity and his only diversified sleeve; the direct Indian book is 56% Vedanta group plus Indian Bank. Folding the money into that book raises concentration.
6. An exit can cost more than it looks: tax (nil if the gain is about Rs 1 lakh, about Rs 0.34 lakh if 13% is annualised over 5 years; rules not verified today) and any units pledged as margin at Upstox.

## Before deciding: what to check

**1. Fund names and categories.** Which funds, which category (large-cap, flexi-cap, mid-cap, hybrid, debt), benchmark and folio start dates. A hybrid or debt fund cannot be judged against the Nifty 50.

**2. Regular or direct plan.** A regular plan pays a distributor commission inside its expense ratio; the direct plan does not. Let P be today's value, n the years, r the regular plan's net return and g the direct plan's advantage (the expense-ratio gap). Then `FV_regular = P × (1 + r)^n` and `FV_direct = P × (1 + r + g)^n`. The table shows `FV_direct - FV_regular` in Rs lakh on Rs 8.5 lakh over 10 years. Switching a regular holding to direct is a redemption plus a fresh purchase, so it can trigger tax and exit load. The no-tax route is to send new instalments to the direct plan and leave old units alone, which keeps the higher fee on them.

**3. Rolling returns against the benchmark.** Use each fund's own benchmark TRI from its factsheet. Compute 3-year and 5-year rolling returns over 7 to 10 years if available, and report the share of windows that beat the benchmark, the median excess return and the worst excess return. Use direct-plan NAVs to judge the manager apart from the fee. Hypothesis: a fund that trails in most windows at both horizons is a replacement candidate; one that trails only over the last 12 months may be in a style cycle.

**4. Overlap with his direct holdings.** Look-through exposure is the fund's weight in a stock times his weight in the fund. Check INDIANB, the Vedanta group (VEDL, VAML, VEDPOWER, VISL, VOGL), ETERNAL, ADANIPOWER, ICICIGI, JIOFIN, IREDA, SUZLON, APOLLO, and the fund's financials, metals, PSU bank and power weights.

**5. Pledged units and margin.** The Portfolio Book marks "Pledged" as Unverified on all 13 Indian lines and records no mutual fund. His notes describe about Rs 25 lakh of margin against pledged stock. Upstox's (or any broker's) treatment of pledged mutual-fund units, the schemes accepted and the haircut were not verified in this run. Ask: are units accepted as collateral, which schemes, what haircut, is redemption blocked while pledged, and what happens to my margin if I redeem. Pull the collateral report from Upstox and ask the registrar (CAMS or KFintech) for lien status per folio.

**6. Exit loads.** Many equity schemes charge about 1% on units redeemed within 12 months of purchase, and each SIP instalment starts its own clock (Not verified; read each scheme's documents). On a 4 to 5 year SIP only the latest 12 or so instalments would normally carry it. Debt, liquid and arbitrage schemes differ.

**Tax-gain harvesting.** Each financial year the first Rs 1.25 lakh of long-term gain is tax-free. Selling units with up to that much gain and buying the same fund back resets the cost to today's NAV. The most it saves in a year is Rs 1.25 lakh × 12.5% = Rs 15,625 (Rs 16,250 with cess). Costs to weigh: exit load on re-bought units, the gap between sale and purchase NAV, and the exemption being shared with all other long-term gains on shares and equity funds that year. His Portfolio Book records full exits of IEX (29 September 2026) and Adani Green (date not stated), with no sale prices, so check whether those used part of it. The same method stages a large exit across two financial years; this one ends 31 March 2027.

## A decision path

1. Establish what 13% is. From the CAS take each fund's XIRR and total gain, plus plan, category, SIP or lump sum, dates, units and pledge status.
2. If 13% is a total gain over 4 to 5 years, the funds trail every comparator in section 2: go to step 3. If it is annualised, the funds are ahead of the comparators (with the SIP caveat): skip to step 6, because the question is role, not performance.
3. If any fund is a regular plan, switching to direct is the first lever: weigh the recurring fee saving against the one-time tax and load, or redirect new instalments.
4. If a fund trails its benchmark in most rolling windows at both 3 and 5 years, replace it within its category. A single weak year is not a reason.
5. If any units are pledged, settle the margin impact with Upstox before redeeming.
6. If the money is meant as his diversified core, do not fold it into the concentrated book. A redemption then needs a diversified destination first. If it is meant as part of the active strategy, treat it as a book decision and measure concentration on the whole Indian equity base.
7. If the gain is within the Rs 1.25 lakh exemption, long-term tax is small; if above, consider staging the exit across 31 March 2027.
8. If the answer is to exit, choose the destination first. Parking earns about the inflation rate: the 5-year FD card is 6.05% against 5.54% CPI (S10, S13), before tax.

Proposal for the weekly review: record the mutual-fund sleeve (fund names, plan type, units, pledge status) in the Portfolio Book so concentration is measured on the whole Indian equity base.
